Place Cells

A place cell is a neuron in the hippocampus that fires when an animal is in one particular place.

Part of an early, unfinished explainers project: very much under construction.

The short version

Place cells were found by recording a neuron's action potentials (Extracellular Electrophysiology) while tracking where the animal is (Behavior Tracking), then simply mapping one against the other: every spike is marked at the spot where the animal was. It's very cool how simple and neat that is. And what comes out is pretty mind-blowing once you think about it: the neuron seems to hold a "map" of locations in the outside world. Because that map is tied to the world rather than to the animal's own point of view (Egocentric vs Allocentric), it is a huge leap in what we can infer about what the brain is computing.
