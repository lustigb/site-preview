How I know Anna

Research mentor, Vanderbilt (2009–12)

I was lucky enough to work with Anna Roe as a research assistant at Vanderbilt, after my undergraduate work there.

Anna is an amazing scientist. She started with amazing work in vision, and she was a pioneer of intrinsic signal optical imaging. Working with her taught me a lot about the fundamentals of imaging, which are important for every imaging modality in science.

Anna is also incredible because she crosses sensory modalities. She studies the brain from the position of the cortical column as a universal structure, which is why she branched into both visual and somatosensory cortex.

This ties back to some of her most famous early work: her paper, where visual input was rewired into auditory cortex (Roe AW, Pallas SL, Hahm JO, Sur M (1990). A map of visual space induced in primary auditory cortex). It showed that cortex maps and forms its fields based on the input it receives. This is a huge finding, and it leads to a lot of important ideas, especially for development, plasticity, and even brain-machine interfaces.

I am grateful that, as a research assistant, she gave me the freedom to push my enthusiasm for optogenetics, and its obvious pairing with the artificial dura and optical windows in monkeys.

Looking forward, she continues to trailblaze by exploring the cortical representation of the fovea. I think this is super cool and important, especially when you think about how vision itself is active sensing: a loop between the senses, movement, and the environment.
